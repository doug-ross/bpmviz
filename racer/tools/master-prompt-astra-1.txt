## PART 7 · TIMING MODEL

All timing numbers use minutes.

Use documented durations first.
Otherwise use explicit estimates, informed by these starting ranges:

- human entry: 15–45
- verification/reconciliation: 30–120

- approval/sign-off: 15–60
- exception handling: 30–90

AFTER, where technically plausible:
- extraction and validation: 2–5
- available digital evidence gathering: 3–10
- assessment and recommendation: 3–8
- authorized routine execution: 1–5

These are modeling assumptions, not benchmarks or guarantees.

### 7.1 Do not manufacture savings

Do not impose a target cycle-time reduction.

Derive improvement from actual proposed changes:
- reduced rekeying
- parallel data gathering

- automated checks
- preassembled evidence
- faster routing
- permitted routine execution

human handling that the design does not actually remove.

### 7.2 Offsets and dependencies

s and e are elapsed process offsets, not labor effort.

- s >= 0
- e >= s
- sequential successors do not start before their required predecessors finish
- parallel branches may overlap
- required joins wait for the longest required branch
- do not add mutually exclusive branch durations together
- do not count queue time twice

Use zero-duration events and gateways unless they include actual modeled work.

### 7.3 Branches and representative baseline

This visualization is not a full branch-execution simulator.

Choose and disclose a comparable representative scenario for before and after.

Prefer the longest modeled acyclic completion route as the displayed baseline
when needed to keep all visible task intervals within the timeline.
If it includes human exception review, label it accordingly.
Do not call that route "typical clean-path performance".

Clean-path and delayed variants can be represented through the five cases.

For rework loops:
- preserve the loop visually
- state the modeled number of passes
- incorporate that effort into the affected stage
- do not create negative or backward-moving time

For sampled QA:
- if shown as a timed pre-release task, include it on the sampled route
- if post-completion, describe it in the relevant detail rather than extending
  the customer-completion baseline with a misleading timed task

All visible node intervals must fit within that view's baseline.

### 7.4 Time basis

Use one consistent convention for both views:
- business minutes, or
- continuous elapsed minutes

State the convention.
For human queues without a calendar, prefer business minutes.
Do not silently mix an eight-hour business day with a 24-hour elapsed day.

## PART 11 · ILLUSTRATIVE METRIC MODEL

Cases are synthetic unless the BPMN explicitly includes actual case data.

Financial and staffing metrics must be labeled illustrative.
Do not infer labor cost from elapsed queue or waiting time.

### Assumption selection

Use documented inputs where available.

Otherwise choose a conservative illustrative scenario appropriate to
the domain, and state:
- annual case volume
- human effort per case before and after
- fully loaded hourly labor rate
- incremental AI/service cost per case
- annual productive hours per FTE

These are scenario assumptions, not measured facts.

Use a common annual volume, hourly rate, and productive-hours assumption
across the five cases.
Allow case-level effort and AI cost to vary with complexity.

### Calculations

Before cost/unit:
(before human-effort minutes / 60 × hourly rate)
+ modeled before nonlabor cost

After cost/unit:
(after human-effort minutes / 60 × hourly rate)
+ modeled after nonlabor cost
+ incremental AI/service cost

Annual modeled operating-cost difference:
(before cost/unit − after cost/unit) × annual volume

FTE-equivalent capacity:
((before human-effort minutes − after human-effort minutes) / 60
× annual volume) / annual productive hours per FTE

Describe FTE as capacity released, not committed headcount reduction.

Describe annual savings as modeled operating-cost difference or capacity value,
not guaranteed cash savings.
Exclude unmodeled implementation costs explicitly.
Do not sum the five case estimates as though they were separate portfolios.

Cycle reduction:
round((b − a) / b × 100)

Do not force:
- a minimum improvement
- $150K–$300K savings
- a 1.2–2.5 FTE outcome
- a positive financial result

### Other metrics

Human touches:
- count meaningful human interventions, not mouse clicks
- state whether averages include exceptions and QA

Straight-through:
- illustrative share completing without human intervention
- if every case requires human authorization, do not claim 100% straight-through

Exception rate:
- share routed outside the routine path
- not synonymous with error rate
- better detection may increase it

Throughput:
- estimate from the limiting capacity and resource assumptions
- do not automatically use baseB / baseA
- format as a multiplier, e.g. "×1.8"
- explain the modeled constraint in assumptions
- use "×1.0" when no increase is justified
